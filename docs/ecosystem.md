# Credential ecosystem alignment

The NIST NCCoE credential series provides informative context for ecosystem roles, formats, issuance and presentation. The following mappings connect those observations to [FRAMEWORK](../spec/bindings/FRAMEWORK-draft-02.md) and the concrete requirements of COMMON, DTI, DSCP and DCP in the CERTCONCORD-governed composition.

CertConcord separates the framework contract from compositions, interfaces, ecosystems and platforms. None gives an external organization implicit authority over a trust domain.

CertConcord derives holder roles and interfaces from its own credential and operation requirements. EUDI is an optional ecosystem mapping alongside independently designed holder architectures. Unrelated implementations need not adopt its component model, trust infrastructure or certification scheme. This architectural independence does not depend on implementing personal wallet portability.

| Layer                | Governing selection                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Framework contract   | Mechanism-independent authority, purpose, operation, lifecycle and evidence requirements in FRAMEWORK                                   |
| Composition profile  | A selected governance and mechanism composition; `certconcord-governed-draft-02` uses COMMON, DTI, DSCP, PRF-KPP, DCP and enabled extensions |
| Open interfaces      | Independently versioned MTC, PKIX/CMS, WebAuthn, OpenID, ISO mdoc and browser mediation adapters                                        |
| Credential ecosystem | A selected national, regional, professional or organizational rulebook, with its type, issuer, reader and assurance policy              |
| Platform             | The chosen browser, wallet API, device key provider or remote service, with exact capabilities and admission evidence                   |

In the CERTCONCORD-governed composition, national identity credentials, Photo ID, EUDI PID and custom mdocs enter the same typed RA evidence boundary. Signing mdocs and MTC certificates use the same governed document-operation model. A better architecture may succeed the current issuance/transparency design and introduce incompatible formats or interfaces. An ecosystem may add requirements for its participants; it does not silently change another ecosystem's rules or historical verification semantics. Protocol editions and holder architectures can evolve independently or together; backward compatibility is not a framework-wide obligation.

## Trust and certification semantics

The RRA model distinguishes an identity-evidence issuer from a signing-credential issuer, even when one organization performs both roles. A government issuer may maintain an IACA/Document Signer hierarchy; the legal organization is not automatically a CA certificate in every relying party's PKIX path.

An RA's acceptance of external identity evidence creates a scoped local approval under that RA's policy. It does not create a cryptographic cross-certificate or delegate signing authority from the source issuer. Conversely, an issuer explicitly trusted for the RRA signing purpose can directly issue a personal signer mdoc. A combined identity/signing credential is possible only when its actual issuer authenticates the additional namespace and the ecosystem permits the profile.

The PQ key certification, credential seal, issuance-log binding, SIM/QTB/ACB and native COSE document profile are RRA composition rules. Their normative definitions are in COMMON, DTI, DSCP and DCP. Ordinary mdoc presentation and document signing retain separate semantics.

## Implementation decisions

1. **Purpose-specific trust.** Identity-source allowlists, signing-issuer policy, reader authority, wallet attestation and activation-attestor authority are distinct registries or typed policy roles. Registering a public key is insufficient without its permitted purpose and scope.
2. **Assurance provenance.** RA decisions retain the source process, evaluation policy, evidence commitment, assessment time and applicable limitations. Government branding, mdoc format, TLS, local biometrics and device-key possession cannot manufacture an unobserved assurance claim.
3. **Explicit status coverage.** Issuer-certificate revocation and individual-credential revocation are separate. The identity adapter records ISSUER_CERTIFICATE/NOT_PROVIDED when only a DS CRL and MSO validity were checked. A PER_CREDENTIAL policy refuses that result. Accepting an issuer/validity-only mode requires an explicit maximum credential lifetime and documented residual exposure.
4. **Bounded claim use.** Requests minimize attributes. A verifier supplies authenticated facts and evidence commitments; the RA or relying party makes the purpose-specific eligibility decision. Undisclosed fields, unknown issuance metadata and absent holder UV evidence stay unknown.
5. **Stable consent.** Identity disclosure consent is separate from approval to issue a signing credential and from consent to sign exact content. Replacing an expired QR/request uses a new nonce and invalidates the old context. It must not silently change the wallet, identity, recipient, document or transport.
6. **Privacy-aware freshness.** Cacheable signed issuer/status material avoids unnecessary issuer observation of every presentation. It does not establish unlinkability: stable holder keys, status indices, credential signatures and subsequent document public keys remain possible correlation handles.
7. **Measured interoperability.** Features are pinned per adapter. OpenID4VP over DC API is not automatically an ISO Annex D conformance claim. A later harmonized protocol requires its own lock, transcript tests and migration reader.

These decisions define the trust, disclosure and consent requirements applied to each enabled wallet and protocol profile.

## Further engineering evidence

The [EUDI ARF 3.0.0 mapping](eudi-architecture.md) evaluates service-scoped authority, wallet/key evidence, external trust lists, transactional data and functional conformance. Its architectural selection is separate from the retained `EUDI_PID_ARF_1_4` wire preset and from independently maintained rulebooks and technical specifications. DTI expresses the reusable authority and lifecycle rules without requiring EUDI deployment.

The [WebAuthn edition guide](webauthn-evolution.md) evaluates Level 4 PRF confidentiality and remote-desktop delegation alongside the baseline authentication and PSCP signing paths. The [Apple integration guide](apple-identity.md) separately addresses operating-system mediation, provider eligibility and the distinction between disclosure and document approval.

The linked [NCCoE usability assessment](https://pages.nist.gov/nccoe-mdl-project-static-website/use-case-1/usability-results.html) reports confusion from QR expiry, changed flows and inconsistent terminology in a small controlled study. Wallet acceptance in the CERTCONCORD-governed composition therefore includes sufficient review time, visible session replacement, cancellation and consistent consent labels. These are deployment UX tests; cryptographic tests alone cannot establish informed user consent.

For operational assurance analysis, the series also links [Building Assurance in the mDL Ecosystem](https://pages.nist.gov/nccoe-mdl-project-static-website/building_mdl_assurance/building_mdl_assurance.html) and the [NCCoE privacy-risk resources](https://pages.nist.gov/nccoe-mdl-project-static-website/pram.html). Applicable published requirements and exact adopted versions must be assessed directly before making external assurance or certification claims.

## Standards integration contract

CertConcord integrates existing standards and develops original profiles, extensions and successor mechanisms. Objects claiming compatibility with an upstream standard retain the semantics of their selected revision. A divergent design defines its own formats, assumptions and verification rules under a distinct binding. CertConcord supplies composition rules for authority, purpose, consent, key execution, lifecycle and evidence across those interfaces. Each original binding identifies its version, additional semantics and deployment conditions. Adoption of a CertConcord extension does not imply adoption or endorsement by an upstream standards organization.
